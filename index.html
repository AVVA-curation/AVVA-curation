Abstract

Integrating audio and visual data for training multimodal foundational models remains a challenge. The Audio-Video Vector Alignment (AVVA) framework addresses this by considering AV scene alignment beyond mere temporal synchronization, and leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) for data curation. AVVA implements a scoring mechanism for selecting aligned training data segments. It integrates Whisper, a speech-based foundation model, for audio and DINOv2 for video analysis in a dual-encoder structure with contrastive learning on AV pairs. Evaluations on AudioCaps, VALOR, and VGGSound demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed model architecture and data curation approach. AVVA achieves a significant improvement in top-k accuracies for video-to-audio retrieval on all datasets compared to DenseAV, while using only 192 hrs of curated training data. Furthermore, an ablation study indicates that the data curation process effectively trades data quality for data quantity, yielding increases in top-k retrieval accuracies on AudioCaps, VALOR, and VGGSound, compared to training on the full spectrum of uncurated data.

From raw pairs to training data

How the curation works

Describe the audio and video separately, then reason about how they fit together. Language guides data selection; AVVA learns directly from the selected audio–video pairs.

  1. Segment the video

    Split input videos into 3-second clips, keeping the audio paired with its video.

  2. Describe each modality

    LLaVA-Next-Video captions the video. LTU-AS describes the audio, including speech and environmental sounds.

  3. Score the alignment

    Mistral compares the descriptions using five criteria: Temporal Alignment, Spatial Coherence, Contextual Relevance, Physical Causality, and Sound Source Visibility. Each score is on a scale of 0–10.

  4. Select, then train

    Average the scores with equal weights and apply a selection threshold. Train AVVA on the retained pairs using Whisper and DINOv2 with contrastive learning.

Cross-modal retrieval

AVVA achieves audio-to-video accuracy comparable to DenseAV, with significantly improved video-to-audio accuracy, despite using only 192 hrs of carefully curated data compared to DenseAV's 5,800 hrs, demonstrating a 30x improvement in data efficiency.

Data curation

A key observation from our experiments is that increasing the amount of training data does not always lead to better performance.

Temporal alignment

The analysis reveals a clear peak in similarity at 0 sec shift between audio and video, providing evidence of meaningful audio-video learning.

The dataset that followed

Dataset

AVE-2 ↗

AVE-2 provides audiovisual captions, alignment scores, score rationales, and sound-source annotations. Access is gated: requests are reviewed individually and require agreement to the dataset’s citation terms.

The annotation layer is attributed to Ali Vosoughi and co-authors under CC BY 4.0. Research using AVE-2 should cite both the AVVA paper and the SoundCLIP preprint, as requested on the dataset card.
